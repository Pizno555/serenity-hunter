# Evidence Ladder

Use this file to grade sources and keep current investment claims grounded.

## Source levels

### Strong evidence

Use for high-confidence conclusions.

- SEC, HKEX, SSE, SZSE, Beijing Stock Exchange, MOPS, TDnet, DART, and local exchange filings.
- Annual reports, interim reports, quarterly reports, official announcements.
- Earnings transcripts and official investor presentations.
- Official customer contracts, order announcements, tender wins, capacity reservations, prepayments.
- Regulatory filings, project approvals, environmental/energy approvals, local government project records.
- Patents, standards documents, technical papers, certification records.

### Medium evidence

Use to support or triangulate.

- Reputable financial media.
- Trade publications.
- Industry association data.
- Company website and product pages.
- Sell-side or specialist research when assumptions are visible.
- Supplier/customer cross-checks from public disclosures.

### Weak evidence

Use as leads. Confirm with stronger sources before making a high-confidence claim.

- KOL posts.
- Social media threads.
- Forum discussions.
- Screenshots with unclear origin.
- Unattributed channel checks.
- Price action or volume spikes without fundamental evidence.

## Claim handling

For current security-specific claims:

- cite or name the source type;
- separate confirmed facts from interpretation;
- mark weak claims as leads;
- avoid building a top-ranked candidate on weak evidence alone.

## Candidate evidence standard

For each final top candidate, aim to include:

- one strong or medium source-backed fact about business position;
- one source-backed fact about demand, capacity, customer validation, financial quality, or valuation;
- the main missing proof;
- the clearest condition that would make the idea weaker.

## Plain-language evidence labels

Use these labels in final answers:

- **Strong**: supported by filings, official announcements, transcripts, regulatory/project documents, or hard technical documents.
- **Medium**: supported by credible media, trade publications, specialist analysis, or cross-company public evidence.
- **Weak**: based on social discussion, rumor, unexplained price action, or early leads.
- **Needs checking**: important and awaiting verification with available tools.

## Red flags

Downgrade confidence when:

- the thesis relies on a single customer rumor;
- the stock moved mainly because of social media attention;
- the company needs financing before the opportunity converts to revenue;
- the customer is unnamed and revenue impact is vague;
- inventories and receivables rise faster than revenue;
- gross margin fails to improve despite claimed scarcity;
- management uses theme language while segment data stays unchanged.

## v2.0.0 guard additions (appended, original content above untouched)

### Unsupported claims guard

For every top-ranked candidate (verdict "Worth tracking" or higher):

- cite at least one strong or medium source for the core business-position claim;
- cite at least one source-backed fact for demand, capacity, customer validation, financial quality, or valuation;
- if no source is available, label the claim "Needs checking" and cap the candidate at "Early lead or low priority";
- never build a top-ranked candidate on weak evidence alone, even if the narrative is compelling.

### Strict evidence-strength mapping

No exceptions for "reputable media" or "well-known fact":

- **Strong** only: exchange filings, annual/interim/quarterly reports, official announcements, earnings transcripts, regulatory/project documents, patents, standards, certification records.
- **Medium** only: reputable financial media, trade publications, industry association data, company IR pages, visible sell-side research, cross-company public disclosures.
- **Weak** only: KOL posts, social media, forums, unattributed channel checks, screenshots with unclear origin, price action without fundamentals.
- **Needs checking**: important claim awaiting verification. Must include the search path (where to look, what document type, what keyword).

### Fabrication prohibition

Never fabricate a source. If a filing, announcement, or number cannot be verified:

- state "Needs checking" explicitly;
- provide the exact search path (exchange, document type, keyword, approximate date);
- do not paraphrase a source you have not read.

## 7-Tier source search priority (appended, original content above untouched)

Use this tier order as the **search priority** when gathering sources. The 3-level labels (Strong/Medium/Weak/Needs checking) above remain the **output labels** for final answers.

### Tier 1: Official financial reports / Announcements

| Source | Evidence strength | Credibility |
|---|---|---|
| Annual reports, interim reports, quarterly reports, official announcements. | Strong | High |
| Earnings transcripts and official investor presentations. | Strong | High |
| Official customer contracts, order announcements, tender wins, capacity reservations. | Strong | High |

### Tier 2: Exchange / Regulatory filings

| Source | Evidence strength | Credibility |
|---|---|---|
| SEC, HKEX, SSE, SZSE, BSE, MOPS, TDnet, DART, and local exchange filings. | Strong | High |
| Regulatory filings, project approvals, environmental/energy approvals. | Strong | High |
| Supplier/customer cross-checks from public disclosures. | Medium | Medium-High |

### Tier 3: Authoritative industry data

| Source | Evidence strength | Credibility |
|---|---|---|
| Patents, standards documents, technical papers, certification records. | Strong | High |
| Trade publications. | Medium | Medium-High |
| Industry association data. | Medium | Medium-High |
| Company website and product pages. | Medium | Medium-High |

### Tier 4: Sell-side research reports

| Source | Evidence strength | Credibility |
|---|---|---|
| Sell-side or specialist research when assumptions are visible. | Medium | Medium-High |

### Tier 5: Financial media

| Source | Evidence strength | Credibility |
|---|---|---|
| Reputable financial media. | Medium | Medium |

### Tier 6: Investment community

| Source | Evidence strength | Credibility |
|---|---|---|
| KOL posts. | Weak | Low (Reference only) |
| Social media threads. | Weak | Low (Reference only) |
| Forum discussions. | Weak | Low (Reference only) |
| Screenshots with unclear origin. | Weak | Low (Reference only) |
| Unattributed channel checks. | Weak | Low (Reference only) |

### Tier 7: Price-volume patterns

| Source | Evidence strength | Credibility |
|---|---|---|
| Price action or volume spikes without fundamental evidence. | Weak | Low (Lead only) |

### Search priority rule

When gathering sources for a deep scan, work through tiers **top-down**:
Tier 1 → Tier 2 → Tier 3 → ... → Tier 7.

Aim to exhaust each tier before moving to the next. Tier 6-7 sources are leads only — always confirm with Tier 1-3 before making a high-confidence claim.

### Cross-verification footnote

Cross-company triangulation from public disclosures (Tier 2) originates from exchange filings but functions as cross-company inference rather than single-company direct disclosure, hence Medium strength. May be upgraded to Strong after cross-verification against 2+ independent sources.
